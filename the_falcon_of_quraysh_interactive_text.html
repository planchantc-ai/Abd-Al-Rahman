The Falcon of Quraysh: The Story of Abd al-Rahman I

Panel 1: Escape from Damascus (750 CE)

In 750 CE, the Umayyad royal family in Syria was overthrown by a rival group called the Abbasids. Young Prince Abd al-Rahman barely escaped with his life. He swam across the cold Euphrates River while his enemies watched from the bank.

Drawing Idea: A young prince swimming across a dark river with soldiers on the bank.

Panel 2: The Journey across North Africa (750 CE – 755 CE)

For five long years, from 750 CE to 755 CE, Abd al-Rahman traveled over 3,000 miles across North Africa disguised as a traveler. He was severely homesick for his Syrian homeland, but his mother's Berber family in North Africa protected him and helped him survive.

Drawing Idea: Abd al-Rahman walking through the desert with a horse, looking tired but determined.

Panel 3: Arrival in Spain (755 CE)

In 755 CE, Abd al-Rahman crossed the Mediterranean Sea and landed in Al-Andalus (Islamic Spain). Spain was divided by local rulers who were constantly fighting. Seeing an opportunity, Abd al-Rahman gathered an army to unite the region.

Drawing Idea: A boat arriving on the shores of Spain with a Islamic flag.

Panel 4: Victory at Córdoba (756 CE)

In 756 CE, Abd al-Rahman won a decisive battle outside the city of Córdoba. He declared himself the Emir (ruler) of Al-Andalus, establishing a new kingdom independent of the Abbasid Caliphate in Baghdad.

Drawing Idea: Abd al-Rahman riding into the walled city of Córdoba as a ruler.

Panel 5: Building a Home Away from Home (784 CE)

In 784 CE, feeling deeply homesick, Abd al-Rahman planted a Syrian palm tree in his palace garden in Córdoba—the first ever in Europe. He also began building the Grand Mosque of Córdoba, using beautiful red-and-white arches inspired by his childhood home in Damascus.

Drawing Idea: Abd al-Rahman planting a palm tree next to grand stone arches under construction.

Panel 6: The Golden Age Begins (788 CE and beyond)

When Abd al-Rahman died in 788 CE, he left behind a peaceful, thriving kingdom. Córdoba grew into one of the world's greatest centers of science, learning, medicine, and architecture, where Muslim, Jewish, and Christian scholars worked together in harmony.

Drawing Idea: Scholars holding books, scrolls, and scientific tools inside a grand library.
